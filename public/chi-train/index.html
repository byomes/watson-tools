The big picture: follow the signal

Sound is a path. It starts at a source, passes through the mixer, and leaves in two directions: to the people in the room, and to the people watching online. When anything goes wrong, walk this path from the left, one step at a time, until you find where the sound stops.

Two exits from the mixer. The top path (amps, speakers) is what the room hears. The bottom path (ATEM, Mac mini, OBS) is what the online audience hears. They can sound different on purpose. Which XR18 output feeds the ATEM is still being confirmed, so the stream bus label above is our best understanding until we trace it together on Saturday.

The gear, one piece at a time

Nothing here is complicated by itself. Each box does one job.

Microphones (stage)

Four wireless mics, each with a Sennheiser receiver in the rack labeled Mic 1 to Mic 4 (exact mic models to confirm). Two thin choir mics hang left and right. Wireless mics need fresh batteries every service and must be switched on before they are unmuted.

Stage box and snake

Mics and instruments plug in on stage. A multi-cable "snake" carries all of those signals to the rack at the back of the sanctuary and into the mixer.

Behringer XR18 mixer

The heart of the sound system. There is no physical board in the room. The XR18 is controlled from the House iPad using the X AIR app (a laptop with X AIR Edit also works). Every source has a channel strip with a name, a gain, a fader and a mute.

Amplifiers

A Behringer NX1000 and a Crown CDi 600 sit in the rack with a power conditioner. They take the mixer's quiet signal and make it strong enough to drive the speakers. They are switched on after the mixer and off before it.

Speakers and subwoofer

The speakers serve the congregation. A large powered subwoofer sits upstage center for low frequencies. Two Eurolive floor monitors and four P16-M personal monitor mixers let the band hear themselves.

ATEM Mini Pro

A Blackmagic video switcher. Both cameras plug into it, and it hands one picture and one sound to the Mac mini over USB. Check that its Stream Audio button is lit.

Mac mini and OBS

The streaming computer. OBS puts the camera picture, overlays and sound together and sends them out through Restream to YouTube and Facebook. A Stream Deck sits by the ATEM monitor: the red "switch cam" key changes cameras.

Lyrics computer and ProPresenter

The Windows PC in the booth runs ProPresenter. It drives three places: the booth monitor, the two sanctuary TVs (same picture on both) and the confidence monitor at the back for the worship team. MultiTracks Playback on the iPad can advance the lyrics automatically with MIDI cues. Manual clicking is always the fallback.

Planning Center

Where the weekly plan and song order live. Playback can pull the song list from the plan so nobody types it twice.

Sunday checklists

These follow the team's own start-up sheets. Fix the order once and keep to it. Where something reads "confirm," we settle it together on Saturday and update the sheet.

A. Sound start-up

  1. Unlock the iPad drawer and take the House iPad.
  2. Run the Kasa app: "First On," then "Second On" to power the system.
  3. Open the X AIR app and connect to the mixer.
  4. Load the snapshot called Sunday 10 am. Never save over it.
  5. Put fresh batteries in the wireless mics; turn them on.
  6. Unmute the mics and instruments you need.
  7. Set up the pastor's headset mic.
  8. Do a quick check of each mic and instrument at low volume, then raise to taste.

B. Lyrics start-up

  1. Turn on the Lyrics monitor and wake the PC.
  2. Open ProPresenter.
  3. Choose the playlist for today's date.
  4. Turn on the front TV and the back (confidence) TV.
  5. Click through the first slides of each song to see them on every screen.
  6. If MIDI cues are working, they advance the slides. If not, click manually and tell the core team afterward.

C. Stream start-up

  1. Turn on the Mac mini and its monitors.
  2. Confirm OBS, the Stream Deck software and Companion are running.
  3. Confirm the ATEM is on and Stream Audio is lit.
  4. Check the YouTube and Facebook titles are right.
  5. Two minutes before service, start the stream and the recording.
  6. Post chat messages and links during the service.
  7. Show the end bumper, then stop the stream and the recording.

D. Wednesday night

  1. Restore the ATEM "Wednesday Night Setup" file for the picture-in-picture layout.
  2. Follow the same sound, lyrics and stream steps as Sunday.
  3. Remember: Planning Center has no upcoming Wednesday plans right now, so the song list may need to be built by hand until that is fixed.

Shut-down (all teams) suggested, confirm Saturday

  1. Stop the stream and recording; confirm the recording saved.
  2. Mute everything on the mixer, then power down the amps before the mixer.
  3. Take the batteries out of the wireless mics and put the mics in their place.
  4. Return the House iPad to the drawer and plug it in; lock the drawer.
  5. Close ProPresenter and turn off the TVs and monitors.

Sound basics for volunteers

Gain versus fader
Gain sets how loud a source is coming into the mixer. Set it once so the level meter peaks near the top but never turns red. The fader is the everyday volume control for the mix. Do not "fix" a quiet mic by pushing gain during the service. Use the fader.
Mute is your friend
Mute any mic that is not in use. An open mic that nobody is using adds noise and invites feedback.
Feedback (the squeal)
It happens when a mic hears its own speaker. Pull the fader down first, then find which mic it is. Fix it with placement (mic away from the speakers) before reaching for any other control.
Snapshots
A snapshot is a saved picture of the whole mixer: names, levels, settings. Sunday 10 am is the working snapshot. Before you change anything big, save a new snapshot with today's date. Do not overwrite the one that works.
Hear what the room hears
Walk to the middle of the room at some point during sound check. What you hear at the mixer, or on headphones, is not what a visitor in row ten hears.
Lesson from this month: when the mixer was reconnected to a computer for the first time, its channel names and levels were wiped and had to be restored from the Sunday 10 am snapshot. That is why a saved scene file and a dated snapshot matter. Save both before you connect a new device or change any routing.

Streaming basics

The stream has three parts: picture (cameras, ATEM), sound (mixer, ATEM) and delivery (OBS, Restream, YouTube, Facebook). If the online audience reports a problem, ask which of the three it is.

Picture problems

  • Wrong camera showing: use the Stream Deck "switch cam" key.
  • Black screen: check the ATEM shows the right input on program.
  • Frozen picture: check OBS is not paused, then the USB cable between ATEM and Mac.

Sound problems

  • No sound online: Stream Audio lit on the ATEM? OBS audio meter moving?
  • Muddy or too quiet: ask what the stream is hearing, not just the room.
  • Echo: someone is watching the stream in the room with the speakers on.

Delivery problems

  • OBS shows "live" but viewers see nothing: check Restream and the platform's own page.
  • Choppy video: close other apps on the Mac mini; it has limited memory.
  • Always record locally too, so the service is not lost.

Chat and links

  • Someone watches chat during service and posts the welcome and links.
  • Use the end bumper, then stop. Do not just close the laptop lid.

When something breaks: start here

Rule one: do not change several things at once. Rule two: go from the source to the speaker, one step at a time. Rule three: if it is mid-service and you are not sure, use the manual fallback and fix it afterward.

Core team Deeper problems

1. Lyric cues that do not fire

A MultiTracks song carries MIDI messages at exact times. Playback sends them across the network, a MIDI driver on the PC receives them, and ProPresenter jumps to a slide.

The chain: Playback on the iPad → network MIDI session → Windows PC → virtual MIDI port → ProPresenter MIDI device → slide.

Test one hop at a time:

  1. Screenshot Playback's MIDI settings (bus, port, channel) and ProPresenter's Devices > MIDI mapping.
  2. On the PC, open MIDI-OX and listen to the incoming port while a cue song plays. Messages arriving means the problem is inside ProPresenter. No messages means look at the network, the driver or Playback.
  3. Read the latency in the network MIDI window. Steady high latency means fix the network, not the software.
  4. Several MIDI drivers are installed on the PC at once. Note what each does, then keep one network MIDI driver and one virtual-port driver.
  5. Rename each iPad so no two share a name in the MIDI session, and clear old peers.
  6. Set ProPresenter transitions to Cut, then test one song end to end.
Check also: rented songs only play on the device they were rented on, and a song with no Production Cues will never fire lyric cues. Confirm which songs are owned and which are rented.

2. Planning Center to Playback: "no plans"

What we found: the import picker in MultiTracks still lists the old service type name, Sunday 10am, while Planning Center now calls it Sunday Morning. Renaming a service type breaks the link until it is re-linked. Wednesday Nights has no future plans to import.

  • Fix: a full Planning Center administrator opens Songs > Actions > Song File Integrations and presses Relink for Sunday Morning, then imports again in Playback.
  • Or give the shared tech login Songs-level permission so it can press Relink itself.
  • Do not fully disconnect the integration; that removes previously attached MultiTracks files from Planning Center.
  • Fallback while it is fixed: build the setlist by hand in Playback so Sunday is safe.

3. Versions and drift between computers

The booth PC, the Mac mini and the laptop run different versions of the same tools (Companion, OBS, NDI). Different versions are a common reason settings go missing or behave differently from one computer to the next. Pick one version of each tool, write it down, and update every machine together, never the week of a big service.

ProPresenter's library lives on the shared drive. Before any ProPresenter update or rollback, copy the whole library, because newer versions can change the library so older ones read it badly.

4. Stream Decks: Companion or Elgato software?

One Stream Deck is controlled by one program at a time. Companion is the better choice when one deck must control OBS, the ATEM, cameras and ProPresenter together. The Elgato plugin is simpler if the deck only drives ProPresenter. Choose one per deck and label it.

5. Better stream audio (proposal)

Create a dedicated auxiliary bus on the XR18 just for the stream, separate from the main output. The main output feeds the amps and speakers. The stream bus feeds the ATEM or OBS. Assign that same bus to the XR18's headphone jack, with a cable to where the stream tech sits, so the person watching the stream hears what viewers hear. First, learn how sound reaches OBS today. Do not change routing until the backup steps below are done.

6. Bus 6 (the stream mix) will not sound different from Main

A bus exists to carry a separate mix. On the XR18, each channel has its own send level to each aux bus, so Bus 6 can be a different mix from Main. If it sounds the same, work down this list. Change one thing at a time and write down what you saw.

  1. Save first. Save a scene file and a new dated snapshot. Do not overwrite "Sunday 10 am."
  2. Is Bus 6 really what is feeding the stream? Open Routing, then the Outputs page. See which mixer output goes to the ATEM. If it is patched from Main L/R, the stream hears Main no matter what Bus 6 is doing. Aux outputs are normally patched to their own bus, so confirm that Aux 6 is the one in use.
  3. Are you actually editing Bus 6? The normal faders control Main. Switch the fader bank to Bus 6 (Sends on Faders) and check the faders now look different from the Main mix. Moving them then changes only the stream.
  4. Check each channel's send. Select a channel, open the Sends tab and read its Bus 6 level. A send at zero or off means that source is missing from the stream. Sends at the same level on every channel make Bus 6 a copy of the room.
  5. Check the tap point. The tap says where the send is taken from. A post-fader send follows the channel fader, so every move for the room also moves the stream. Pre-fader keeps the stream steady while the room changes. The globe icon applies a tap change to every channel at once.
  6. Check for a stereo link. Linked buses, such as 5 and 6, move together and share one mix.
  7. Check the Main assignment. A channel's LR button decides whether it also goes to the room. Sources only the stream needs (a talkback or a click track you do not want in the room) can have LR turned off.
  8. Compare after a restore. Loading a snapshot, or reconnecting a computer, can change send levels. Re-check Bus 6 after either.
  9. Listen where the stream is heard. Send Bus 6 to the headphone jack, or solo the bus, to hear it without the room's sound.
Not a hardware limit. We have not yet confirmed which setting is causing this on your mixer. Steps 2 and 5 are the likeliest. We will confirm on Saturday and update this page.

How many different mixes can the XR18 make?

  • Main L/R: one stereo mix for the room (normally also the base for the amps).
  • 6 aux buses: six independent mixes. They can be mono, or linked in pairs for stereo, which gives up to three stereo mixes.
  • 4 effects sends: built for reverb and delay, not for monitor mixes.
  • P16-M personal mixers: the XR18 also feeds the personal monitor mixers over its Ultranet outputs. Each one lets a musician set their own blend of up to 16 sources.

A different mix, not just a different volume? Yes. Each aux bus has its own send level for every channel, so the guitar can be loud in one mix, quiet in another and absent from a third. It also has its own EQ and dynamics, and a stereo bus has its own panning. What is shared is each channel's gain, mute and channel EQ. Those happen before the sends, so a change there affects every mix unless the send is tapped before that point.

Backups and change rules

The single best habit: back up, then change, then test.

  1. Mixer: save a scene file from X AIR Edit and a new dated snapshot. Never overwrite "Sunday 10 am."
  2. OBS: export the scene collection and the profile.
  3. Companion: export the full configuration.
  4. ProPresenter: copy the library and the playlists from the shared drive.
  5. ATEM: save the setup file, including the Wednesday Night Setup.
  6. Playback: note which songs are owned or rented, and screenshot the MIDI settings.
  7. Keep the exports in one shared folder with the date in the file name.
Passwords: keep logins in a password manager, not printed on sheets under keyboards. If a login has been on paper where visitors could see it, change it. This guide intentionally contains no passwords.
About AI tools: they can help, but check their work before it touches a live machine. Try any suggested change on a spare computer first, write down exactly what was changed, and apply it to the booth machines together as a team.

Glossary

Channel
One input strip on the mixer: one mic or one instrument.
Gain
Input level into the mixer. Set once, then use the fader.
Fader
The slider that sets a channel's volume in the mix.
Bus / aux
A separate mix sent somewhere else, such as monitors or the stream.
Main L/R
The main output of the mixer, which goes to the amps and speakers.
Snapshot / scene
A saved picture of the whole mixer's settings.
Feedback
The squeal from a mic hearing its own speaker.
Snake
A multi-channel cable from the stage to the rack.
ATEM
The video switcher that takes the cameras and sends one picture to the Mac mini.
OBS
The software that builds the live stream picture and sound.
Restream
A service that sends one stream to several places at once, such as YouTube and Facebook.
ProPresenter
The lyrics and slides software on the booth PC.
Playback
MultiTracks' app for playing song tracks, which can send MIDI cues.
MIDI cue
A tiny timed message inside a song that tells ProPresenter to change slides.
NDI
A way to send video over the network instead of a cable.
Companion
Software that turns a Stream Deck into a control panel for many devices.
Confidence monitor
The screen facing the worship team so they can read lyrics.
